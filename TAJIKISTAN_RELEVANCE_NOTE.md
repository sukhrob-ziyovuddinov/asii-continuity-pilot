# ASII Tajikistan — Relevance Note

## Purpose

This note explains why a controlled ASII evaluation may be relevant to a regulated financial institution in Tajikistan and what an institution should validate before any next-stage commitment.

It is a discussion and evaluation aid. It is **not** legal advice, a statement of regulatory approval, evidence of institutional adoption, or a claim of production readiness.

## Executive relevance

Financial-crime investigations often require teams to connect changing signals, evidence, analyst reasoning, reviews, and reporting preparation across time. When those elements remain fragmented across alerts, documents, messages, spreadsheets, and case tools, an authorized reviewer may struggle to reconstruct:

- what was known at a specific point in time;
- which evidence supported or contradicted a working hypothesis;
- who performed or reviewed a material action;
- why a disposition was reached;
- what changed when new information arrived;
- whether the final narrative remains traceable to the underlying record.

ASII is designed to test whether a continuity layer can make that chain easier to review without replacing existing systems of record or regulated decision-makers.

## Tajikistan evaluation hypothesis

For a Tajikistan institution, the first question is not whether ASII can replace an AML/CFT, transaction-monitoring, screening, KYC/KYB, or case-management platform. The bounded question is:

> Can ASII improve investigation continuity, evidence traceability, accountable human review, and reporting preparation around the institution's existing workflow?

This hypothesis must be validated with the institution's Financial Monitoring or AML/CFT owner and, where required, Compliance, Risk, Information Security, Technology, Legal, and Data Protection stakeholders.

## Priority use cases to test

A controlled evaluation should select one narrow use case. Candidate examples include:

1. **Alert-to-investigation continuity**  
   Preserve the source alert, linked context, analyst actions, review points, and final disposition in one reviewable chain.

2. **KYC/KYB change review**  
   Show how new ownership, control, business-profile, or risk information changes an existing assessment while preserving the earlier decision context.

3. **Sanctions or adverse-signal escalation**  
   Record the signal received, evidence checked, false-positive or escalation reasoning, reviewers, and subsequent updates without automating the regulated decision.

4. **Transaction-monitoring investigation support**  
   Connect synthetic transaction patterns, supporting documents, analyst hypotheses, contradictory evidence, and human disposition.

5. **Reporting-preparation traceability**  
   Produce a reviewable draft narrative whose material statements can be traced to controlled evidence and recorded human decisions.

These are evaluation examples only. The institution must choose the lawful, operationally useful use case.

## What ASII should demonstrate

The evaluation should make the following visible to an authorized reviewer:

- provenance of each synthetic source signal;
- evidence linked to the investigation;
- chronology of material events and updates;
- actor and role for material actions;
- working hypothesis and unresolved questions;
- evidence supporting and contradicting the hypothesis;
- human review, escalation, and disposition;
- preservation of earlier decision context after new information arrives;
- traceability from reporting-preparation output back to evidence.

## What remains institution-specific

ASII must not assume that one generic workflow satisfies a Tajikistan institution's obligations or internal policies. Before any controlled PoC or pilot, the institution should validate:

- applicable legal and regulatory requirements;
- internal AML/CFT and Financial Monitoring procedures;
- reporting forms, approval paths, and responsible roles;
- data classification and confidentiality requirements;
- permitted hosting and data-location model;
- authentication, authorization, and segregation-of-duties controls;
- logging, audit, retention, deletion, and legal-hold requirements;
- incident response and breach-notification responsibilities;
- procurement, outsourcing, vendor-risk, and information-security review;
- language requirements for users, evidence, and outputs;
- integration boundaries and systems of record.

Any gap identified through this validation becomes an explicit evaluation finding, not an implied capability.

## Controlled evaluation boundary

The first demonstration should use synthetic data and the minimum necessary access.

It must not require:

- production customer or transaction data;
- live KYC/KYB, sanctions, or investigation evidence;
- production credentials or privileged service accounts;
- write access to institution-controlled production systems;
- autonomous customer-impacting or regulated decisions;
- autonomous regulatory filing;
- broad historical-data migration.

Institution-controlled data or environments may be introduced only through a separately approved arrangement defining purpose, roles, fields, access, hosting, retention, deletion, audit, incident handling, and accountable owners.

## Participants

A minimal institution-side evaluation group may include:

- Financial Monitoring / AML/CFT owner;
- investigator or transaction-monitoring representative;
- Compliance or MLRO function;
- Information Security / Technology representative where required;
- Risk, Legal, Data Protection, or Internal Audit where the institution's governance requires them.

The institution decides the final participants and decision rights.

## Ten-minute evaluation conversation

A first institutional session can remain narrow:

1. Confirm one investigation-continuity problem.
2. Confirm the current system of record and human decision owner.
3. Select one synthetic use case.
4. Walk through signal, evidence, investigation, review, disposition, and reporting preparation.
5. Identify security, data, integration, and governance gaps.
6. Agree whether a technical review, controlled PoC scoping step, or no further action is appropriate.

A meeting alone is not success. A meaningful stage transition requires both an identified institutional owner or evaluation team and an official institutional follow-up requesting or agreeing to a next technical, security, evaluation, controlled-PoC, or pilot-consideration step.

## Evaluation questions

The institution should be able to answer:

- Does the workflow reduce fragmentation without duplicating or displacing the system of record?
- Can an authorized reviewer reconstruct what was known, when, why, and by whom?
- Are human decision rights explicit and enforceable?
- Is evidence provenance preserved across updates?
- Are contradictions and unresolved questions visible?
- Can reporting-preparation statements be traced to the record?
- Are access, logging, retention, deletion, and export controls adequate for the proposed scope?
- What must change before any use of institution-controlled data?
- Is the value sufficient to justify a bounded next step?

## Evidence required for the next stage

A controlled evaluation should produce:

1. an agreed use case and owner;
2. a documented data and environment boundary;
3. a synthetic end-to-end demonstration record;
4. evidence-lineage and human-review output;
5. recorded fit, gaps, risks, and required controls;
6. a written next-step decision.

No production, procurement, adoption, regulatory-approval, or performance claim should be made from the evaluation alone.
